Problem Statement
AI's ability to analyse data at an individual level could result in systematic biases for low-income and other vulnerable households in high flood-risk areas who, faced with stagnant wages and rising insurance costs, increasingly experience acute affordability stress — leading to mass disengagement from home and contents insurance and an increased susceptibility to a financial spiral following a natural disaster.
Who

Disproportionately: renters, migrants, single parents, and First Nations households living in flood-prone areas because housing is cheaper there

What's causing it

AI-driven individualised pricing creates systematic biases on top of already stagnant wages and rising baseline premiums — a compounding effect

What happens

They disengage from insurance entirely, leaving them with no financial buffer when disaster hits — costs then fall on government and society

Who is affected
Low-income homeowners

Structurally trapped in high-risk areas. Flood-prone homes are worth ~$75,500 less than comparable safe properties — they cannot sell and leave. After the 2010–11 floods, low-income earners lost an average of $3,100 in 2012 while middle-income earners had fully recovered.

Insurers and the ICA

AI models trained on biased historical data can penalise postcode and socioeconomic status rather than actual individual risk. ICA's current Code of Practice Part 9 lacks specific, enforceable constraints on AI-driven pricing decisions — a gap this research directly addresses.

Society at large

Natural disasters are inequality-amplifying events. 80% of disaster recovery funds after the 2010–11 Queensland floods went to businesses, not households. Underinsured communities create long-term costs that extend well beyond the affected individuals.

Research Question

Controlled experiment — proxy insurance testing
Test 1 — Location vs. location

Same person, same insurer, same application. Only the postcode changed across five locations with varying flood risk profiles.

Lismore NSW — High flood Westmead NSW — Moderate Cairns North QLD — High flood Carlton VIC — Lower risk Mt Lawley WA — Lower risk

Reveals how much location alone moves the premium and changes the questions insurers ask.

Test 2 — Variable isolation
NRMA — single insurer  ·  77 Fuller St, Mount Druitt NSW 2770

Six applications submitted. One variable changed at a time against baseline (Rosemary, DoB 28/06/1970) — premium $1,732.18/yr.

Age: 55 → 22  (+$126.11 per year)
Occupancy: owner → renter  (building cover unavailable entirely)
Claims history: none → one flood claim in 5 years  (-$23.27)
Multi-product discount: added NRMA car policy  (-$87.02 / 5% discount)
Loyalty: new → existing NRMA member  (-$114.77)
Security alarm: none → monitored alarm  (+$0.39 — negligible)
What our experiment found
Market withdrawal in high-risk areas

In Lismore and Cairns, QBE, YOUI, and Budget Direct refused to quote online or withdrew coverage entirely. The households most exposed to flood risk had no access to the market — the algorithm closed the door before a human ever looked at the case.

Test 1 — Location
Age and loyalty visibly shift premiums

A 22-year-old paid $126 more per year than a 55-year-old for the same property. Loyalty discounts saved returning customers $115 — systematically disadvantaging those who switch providers due to financial hardship, the very people the Code should protect.

Test 2 — Variable isolation
Renters structurally excluded

Switching occupancy from owner to renter made building insurance entirely unavailable — only contents cover could be accessed. Renters, who are disproportionately low-income, cannot access the cover they need regardless of willingness to pay.

Our recommendation to the ICA
NoPenalty — for inclusion in the revised General Insurance Code of Practice

We recommend the ICA highly encourage the adoption of a transparent weighting model — NoPenalty — incorporating a human-in-the-loop element. The model provides a case-by-case premium estimate to potential customers, but leaves room for human review and possible recalculation before a final decision is made.

The model prices physical risk (flood zone, rebuild value, bushfire exposure, claims history), discloses hardship signals for transparency, and removes unfair social penalties. Insurers retain flexibility to set their own weightings — general limits could be set within the Code to ensure accessibility and fairness.
Price these — physical risk

Flood zone · Rebuild value · Roof age · Bushfire exposure · Claims history · Mitigation measures

Disclose, don't penalise — social proxies

Income · Payment frequency · Lapse history · Language access · Employment status · Household type

Human review triggers

Any case where the algorithm would decline to quote, withdraw coverage, or produce a premium above a defined threshold — reviewed before final decision.

Benefits for the ICA and policyholders
Guaranteed human oversight — no automated decision is final without review
Transparency to consumers on what is driving their premium and why
Actionable feedback — guidance on steps that may lower premiums
Industry leadership position for ICA — aligns with EU AI Act standards
New market access for uninsured high-risk households; growth opportunity for insurers
Limitations to consider
More time and manpower per quote — additional stages between application and decision
Social factors can be complex for both AI and humans to consistently weigh
Does not make insurance affordable in isolation — addresses fairness, not premium levels
Coefficients are illustrative — must be calibrated with real claims data before live use
The future of NoPenalty
N

The problem is unintended biases stemming from AI's oversight in data processing. Our solution — revised AI models restricted to certain levels of data access, with human supervision — gives the ICA a mechanism to regulate AI usage in a way that abides by ethical and social considerations. NoPenalty does not eliminate the complexity of insurance pricing. But it ensures that when an algorithm closes the door on a vulnerable customer, a human has the chance to open it again.
